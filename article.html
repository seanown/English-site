Why Macau Should Be The Free Port

By Sean Own · Dec 7, 2025 · 5 min read

Every operator knows the feeling. You see a land-grab before the crowd does, and the only question is whether you move. For Chinese enterprises trying to go global, the land-grab is happening right now — and the port they should sail out of is Macau.

People sleep on Macau. They see casinos and nothing else. I see a free port with two computing centers, a bridge to the Greater Bay Area, and a cultural fluency with the West that the mainland can't fake. That is leverage. That is the kind of underpriced attention I have spent my whole career hunting for.

The Dual-Computing Play

With twin computing centers in Macau and Taiwan, we open a compliant, efficient AI pathway for Chinese enterprises to reach the world. Not a workaround. A real, defensible architecture. I introduced the Longyou dual-computing model at the 16th Asian Electronics Forum, and the room got it immediately — Wang Ning, President of the China Electronic Chamber of Commerce, included.

"Legacy is greater than currency. Build the port, and the ships will come."

Here is the part most people miss: going global is not a marketing campaign. It is infrastructure. You need the compliance, you need the compute, you need the associations that shake hands on both sides of the strait. I chair the GBA E-Commerce Association and I run the Longyou AI going-global platform because that is the only way this actually ships.

Culture Is The Product

The West does not just want Chinese goods. It wants Chinese culture — and it has no idea how to reach it. That is why I built the Macao Silk Culture & Art Center and the Macao Culture IP Copyright Center. We take heritage, protect it, and turn it into living, commercial IP. Heritage that sits in a vault creates no legacy. Heritage that travels does.

So here is my thesis, and I will say it the way I say everything: wealth grows from the right way, and profit comes through what is just. Macau is the free port. The question is not whether — it is who moves first.

Source: Macau Daily — 16th Asian Electronics Forum coverage (Dec 7, 2025)
