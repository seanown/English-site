Ninety-Eight Things Without English Names

The first entry here is not a masterpiece. It is a list.

← All Curated
01

What I Selected

Macao's Intangible Cultural Heritage Inventory. On 5 August 2026 the Cultural Heritage Bureau added 28 items; together with the 2017 and 2020 batches, the inventory now holds 98. The notice groups them by type: performance, social and religious practice, knowledge of nature, and traditional craft.

A note on accuracy: the official notice is published in Chinese. The renderings below are my working English, not official titles.

02

Why I Chose It

Not because it is the rarest thing I could find, but because it is among the least visible. Most English-language writing about Macao's heritage begins and ends with the Historic Centre. This list begins somewhere else — with what people here still cook, sing, carve and make.

A city is easy to admire and harder to keep reading about. I would rather start with the second.

03

The Hardest Line To Translate

Yam cha — the teahouse habit: tea, dim sum, a table, a morning. "Tea drinking" is accurate and loses all of it.

Zongzi making — wrapping glutinous rice in leaves. "Rice dumpling" is accurate and still wrong.

Pork chop bun making — a name that already sounds translated, and still needs a sentence before anyone can taste it.

So the rule for this column is simple: keep the local word, then add the sentence. Do not replace it with a tidy English label. That is most of what a translator who is also a curator actually does.

04

The Source You Can Check

05

Edition And Revenue

No edition exists for this entry. When an item here becomes a limited silk edition, the edition terms and where the revenue goes will be written on this page before anything is offered. Until then this is a reading note, not a product.
